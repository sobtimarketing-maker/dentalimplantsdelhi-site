Who basal implants are for

I suggest basal implants only when regular implants are not possible. That is usually because the jaw bone has shrunk after years of missing teeth or wearing a denture, and the patient doesn't want a bone graft or a sinus lift first.

If your scan shows that conventional implants or All-on-4 or All-on-6 can work for you, I will tell you, because they are often the better long-term choice.

Basal vs conventional implants

  • Where they sit

    Conventional implants sit in the upper layer of the jaw. Basal implants are anchored in the deeper basal bone, which stays dense even after the upper bone has shrunk.

  • Bone grafting

    Conventional implants may need a graft where bone is thin. Basal implants are designed to avoid one. When is a bone graft needed?

  • Time to fixed teeth

    With conventional implants the final teeth often follow after a few months of healing. With basal implants, fixed teeth go on within 72 hours.

How treatment works

  • 1. Scan and plan

  • 2. Surgery

    The basal implants are placed in a single surgery.

  • 3. Fixed teeth in 72 hours

    A fixed set of teeth is attached within 72 hours, so you leave with teeth you can smile and eat with.

  • Who needs basal implants?
