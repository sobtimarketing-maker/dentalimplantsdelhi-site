Lower denture keeps slipping? How two implants can hold it

Dr Pushkar Sobti
All-on-X implantologist, Delhi

If you wear a full lower denture, you probably know the routine. It sits fine when your mouth is still, then lifts the moment you start eating or laughing. Some people stop eating certain foods. Some keep a hand near their mouth when they talk. Many use denture glue every day and still don't trust it.

The lower denture is nearly always the worse of the two, and there's a simple reason for it.

Why the lower one moves so much

An upper denture covers the roof of your mouth, and that large surface gives it some suction. A lower denture has no such luck. It sits on a narrow ridge of gum, with your tongue right behind it and your cheeks and lips on either side, all moving whenever you speak or chew.

After teeth are lost, the bone underneath also slowly shrinks, so the ridge gets lower and the denture has even less to hold on to. That's why a denture that fitted well ten years ago may feel loose today, even if nothing about it has changed.

How two implants hold a denture

With implant supported dentures, we place implants in the lower jaw, usually towards the front where the bone tends to be thickest. Each implant gets a small attachment on top, and the denture has matching fittings on its underside. When you put the denture in, it clips onto the implants.

For many people two implants are enough to stop the lower denture lifting. Some cases need more, or a bar joining the implants, and the scan tells me which. Our own lab inside the clinic makes these dentures, so the fit to the attachments is checked here before it reaches you.

You still take the denture out at night to clean it and the implants. What changes is that during the day it stays where it should.

Clip-on denture or fixed teeth?

There are two ways to use implants when all the teeth in a jaw are gone. One is the clip-on denture I've just described. The other is a fixed set of teeth screwed onto the implants, like All-on-4, which you never take out.

A clip-on denture uses fewer implants and suits people who are mostly bothered by movement and are happy to keep removing a denture. Fixed teeth feel closer to your own teeth and there's no plate in your mouth, but it is a bigger treatment. Neither is the right answer for everyone, and I'll go through both with you.

What it involves

We start with a CBCT scan, which shows how much bone there is and where the implants can safely go. The implants are placed in a short procedure under local anaesthetic. Once they've settled into the bone, the attachments go on and the denture is fitted to them.

How much it involves depends mainly on the number of implants and whether we make a new denture or a bar. Once the doctor has seen your scan, our front desk will go through the cost with you. Questions

  • What are implant supported dentures?

    They are dentures that clip onto implants placed in the jaw, so they stay steady while you eat and talk. You still take them out to clean them.

  • How many implants are needed to hold a lower denture?

    For many people two implants are enough to stop a lower denture lifting. Some need more, or a bar between the implants. The CBCT scan shows which.

  • Is a clip-on denture the same as fixed teeth?

    No. A clip-on denture is removable and uses fewer implants. Fixed teeth such as All-on-4 are screwed onto the implants and are not taken out.
